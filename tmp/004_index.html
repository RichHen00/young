Winter gales and ancient headlands

The Gravity of a Static Place

In a landscape of wind-bent trees and stone effigies, a boy finds a strange comfort in the repetition of dead men's names and the ghosts of shipwrecked sailors.

Bude is ennobled by its cliffs, its thundering surf and its eerie hinterland. You can ramble for hours across headlands that run back forming broad, high land on which scattered farms seem settled hull down in wriggling lanes to escape winter gales that have forced the trees to grow almost parallel to the earth. On the gravestones the same names appear over and over, century after century: Mutton, Sleeman, Oke and Prust. Parson Hawker ate opium and wrote outrageous poetry when he wasn’t burying drowned sailors between 1834 and 1878.
