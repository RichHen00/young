Damp floorboards and mouse droppings

The Ghostly Pull of the Wreckers’ Coast

A child’s bedroom overlooks the Atlantic, where the scent of old suitcases mingles with a coastal history of shipwreck and prayer. Here, the horizon promises a distant, dangerous world.

Once, poor Cornish children in these parts prayed, ‘God save Father and Mother and zend a ship to shore vore mornin.’ And on cold, rainy days there always seemed to me to be an aura here of doomed ships and silent watchers on terrible cliffs – an aura that survives today’s asphalted roads and trailer parks.

The attic of the gaunt and ugly Edwardian house smelled of damp floorboards, old suitcases and mouse droppings, but it was dark and large – ideal for hide-and-seek – and full of books, some of which had been my father’s when he was a boy.
