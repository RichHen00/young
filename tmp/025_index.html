Slow Boats to China

Young grew up in a landscape of wind-bent trees and ancient graveyards. In the salt air of Bude, among the ruins of drowned sailors and the ghosts of eccentric vicars, a boy’s hunger for the horizon first took root.

Bude is ennobled by its cliffs, its thundering surf and its eerie hinterland. You can ramble for hours across headlands that run back forming broad, high land on which scattered farms seem settled hull down in wriggling lanes to escape winter gales that have forced the trees to grow almost parallel to the earth. In long, deep valleys you come across small, ancient churches oddly far from any village, and smelling of flowers and grass. On the gravestones the same names appear over and over, century after century: Mutton, Sleeman, Oke and Prust. As a boy, I was particularly fond of a clifftop church at a village called Morwenstow because it had a ship’s figurehead in its graveyard, and because a once-famous and eccentric vicar is buried there. Parson Hawker ate opium and wrote outrageous poetry when he wasn’t burying drowned sailors between 1834 and 1878.

— GAVIN YOUNG
