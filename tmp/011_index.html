A lifelong yearning for escape takes root in the "cosily creepy" atmosphere of the Wreckers’ Coast. Between the effigies of knights and the pound of the sea, Gavin Young transforms a childhood haunting into a deliberate adult adventure.

It was a simple idea: take a series of ships of many sizes and kinds; go where they lead for a few months; see what happens. It was an adaptation of the old idea of Running Away to Sea, a boyhood yearning bred of tales, marvellous tales Of ships and stars and isles where good men rest.

No doubt my dream of the sea was born during the long summers I spent as a child in what I still think of as the oldest-feeling, most soul-subduing and cosily creepy part of England, the part almost hidden between Britain’s battered kneecap and shin. This is the Wreckers’ Coast: a place of buzzards and seals and effigies of knights in dim, half-lost churches, where seas pound into cliffbound bays that have swallowed seamen from a hundred wrecked schooners and, in wartime, perhaps harboured German U-boats.

— Slow Boats to China, GAVIN YOUNG
