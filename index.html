The Lure of a Weathered Grave

In the salt-stung air of Cornwall, a young boy wanders among leaning trees and lonely churches, finding a strange kinship with the ghosts of shipwrecked men.

Bude is ennobled by its cliffs, its thundering surf and its eerie hinterland. You can ramble for hours across headlands that run back forming broad, high land on which scattered farms seem settled hull down in wriggling lanes to escape winter gales that have forced the trees to grow almost parallel to the earth. In long, deep valleys you come across small, ancient churches oddly far from any village, and smelling of flowers and grass. On the gravestones the same names appear over and over, century after century. Parson Hawker ate opium and wrote outrageous poetry when he wasn’t burying drowned sailors between 1834 and 1878.

— Slow Boats to China
