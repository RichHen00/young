The Wreckers’ Coast in summer

The Boyhood Yearning for a Vanishing Horizon

Young grows up on a coastline of salt-crusted stone and shipwrecked schooners. In the shadow of dim churches, a child's curiosity turns into a plan for escape.

It was a simple idea: take a series of ships of many sizes and kinds; go where they lead for a few months; see what happens. It was an adaptation of the old idea of Running Away to Sea, a boyhood yearning bred of tales, marvellous tales Of ships and stars and isles where good men rest.

No doubt my dream of the sea was born during the long summers I spent as a child in what I still think of as the oldest-feeling, most soul-subduing and cosily creepy part of England. This is the Wreckers’ Coast: a place of buzzards and seals and effigies of knights in dim, half-lost churches, where seas pound into cliffbound bays.
